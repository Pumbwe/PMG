Elimination Reactions

An elimination reaction is a type of organic reaction in which a pair of atoms or groups of atoms are removed from an organic molecule. Elimination reaction is the principal process by which saturated organic compounds are converted to unsaturated organic compounds.

Examples

CH3-CH2-Br + alc. KOH → CH2=CH2 + KBr + H2O
CH3-CH-CH3 (Br) + alc. KOH → CH3-CH=CH2 + KBr + H2O

Types of Elimination Reactions

  1. E2 Reaction
  2. E1 Reaction

E2 Reaction

Elimination bimolecular reaction. Occurs with strong base.

Kinetics

Rate = k [RX][Base]

Mechanism

One-step process: base abstracts β-H while leaving group departs simultaneously.

Energy Profile

Orientation & Reactivity

Follows Zaitsev’s rule: more substituted alkene predominates.

Stability order: R2C=CHR > R2C=CH2 > RCH=CHR > RCH=CH2 > CH2=CH2

E1 Reaction

Elimination unimolecular reaction. Common in secondary/tertiary halides with weak base.

Kinetics

Rate = k [RX]

Mechanism

  1. Ionization: RX → carbocation + X⁻ (slow, rate-determining)
  2. Deprotonation: carbocation loses β-H → alkene (fast)

Orientation

Also follows Zaitsev’s rule. Major product = more substituted alkene.

Reactivity Order

3° > 2° >> 1°

Rearrangements

Carbocation rearrangements (hydride/methyl shifts) may occur to form more stable carbocation, leading to different alkene products.
