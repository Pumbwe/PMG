Quick notes — Organic & Electronic Effects

Localized vs Delocalized bonds: Localized electrons sit in a single bond or lone pair; delocalized electrons (π systems) spread across several atoms and stabilize molecules (e.g., benzene).

Inductive effect: Electron withdrawal or donation transmitted through σ bonds due to electronegativity differences (–I or +I). It weakens with distance and affects acidity and stability.

Hyperconjugation: Stabilization by overlap of σ (C–H/C–C) with adjacent empty/partially filled p or π orbitals — explains carbocation stability (3° > 2° > 1°).

Example: Tertiary carbocations are stabilized by more hyperconjugative C–H bonds than primary carbocations.

Quick notes — Analytical Chemistry

Analytical chemistry is the science of chemical measurement. It splits into qualitative (what is present) and quantitative (how much). Classical methods (gravimetric, volumetric) and instrumental methods (spectroscopy, chromatography) are both essential.

Example: Use a UV‑Vis calibration curve to determine unknown concentration from measured absorbance.

Careers: QC labs, environmental monitoring, forensics, pharma, food testing, research.
